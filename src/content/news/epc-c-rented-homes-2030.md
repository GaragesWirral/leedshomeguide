The government has confirmed that privately rented homes in England and Wales must meet a higher energy standard, equivalent to EPC C, from 1 October 2030. Today the legal minimum is E. The decision was published on 21 January 2026 as part of the Warm Homes Plan.

## What has been decided

- **One deadline.** All private tenancies, new and existing, must meet the new standard by 1 October 2030.
- **A new way of measuring.** Homes must first meet a fabric standard (insulation, windows and so on), then either a heating system standard or a smart readiness standard.
- **A £10,000 cap.** If a home cannot reach the standard after £10,000 of improvements, the landlord can register an exemption that lasts 10 years.
- **A current C still counts.** A home rated C or above on today's EPC before 1 October 2029 is treated as compliant until that EPC expires.
- **Short term lets are excluded** for now.

## Why it matters in Leeds

Leeds has many older terraced and back to back homes. Solid walls and older heating systems tend to pull EPC ratings down, so it is worth checking now how far your rental is from a C.

## What landlords can do now

1. **Check the current EPC** on the government register and note its expiry date. A C rating secured before October 2029 lasts until the certificate runs out.
2. **Fix damp first.** Insulation added to a damp wall can make problems worse. A [damp proofing specialist](/trades/damp-proofing/) can tell you what is causing it.
3. **Plan the heating.** An old boiler drags the rating down. Our [new boiler cost guide](/guides/new-boiler-cost/) covers replacement prices, and heat pump grants and loans may change the sums.
4. **Keep receipts.** Spending on qualifying improvements counts towards the £10,000 cap, so keep invoices for every job.

If you let in one of Leeds' licensing areas, the council's licence conditions apply as well: see [selective licensing in Leeds](/guides/selective-licensing-leeds/).
