Every private landlord in England will have to register themselves and each home they let on a new government service called Register your rental property. It is the second phase of the Renters' Rights Act, and it rolls out one region at a time. Yorkshire and the Humber, which includes Leeds, is fifth in line.

## Key dates for Leeds

- **15 April 2027:** the requirement starts for properties in Yorkshire and the Humber.
- **14 July 2027:** the deadline to register.

The deadline follows the property, not where the landlord lives. A Leeds flat owned by a landlord in London still has the Yorkshire deadline. Landlords can register early once the service opens on 15 December 2026, which helps if you let homes in more than one region.

## What it costs

The fee is £65 per property, paid every year. The government says the fee is reduced during the rollout so early registrants do not pay more. Registering yourself as a landlord is free.

## What you will need

As well as your own details, you will be asked for each property's address, type, bedrooms, rent, tenancy and licence details, plus safety records:

- the gas safety record and its date, if the home has gas
- the electrical certificate (EICR or EIC) and its expiry date
- the EPC, and any energy efficiency exemption

After the deadline, the council can take enforcement action, and the government warns that landlords who do not register risk a fine.

Check your certificates are in date before April. If an EICR is due, see our [EICR cost guide](/guides/eicr-cost/) or find a [Leeds electrician](/trades/electricians/).
